# SAME SEED — Dramatic Gates

The screenplay advances only when a dramatic condition has been earned. Technology can escalate at any time; **the story cannot**.

## Gate 0 — THE ORDINARY WORLD MUST WORK

**Question:** Would we watch Evan if no speculative premise ever arrived?

Pass condition:
- Evan, Claire, Mara, Ray and Julian feel like people rather than exposition delivery systems.
- Evan's AI hobby is charming before it is worrying.
- The marriage already contains tenderness and strain unrelated to technology.
- The audience sees why Evan likes making useless things.

Failure mode:
- Opening feels like a “before the singularity” montage.

## Gate 1 — THE MACHINE MUST SURPRISE WITHOUT BECOMING MAGIC

**Question:** Can a small AI interaction create genuine dissonance while remaining technically ordinary?

Pass condition:
- An early model completes an emotional thought Evan had not yet phrased.
- Evan is delighted, not terrified.
- Claire can plausibly dismiss it as pattern matching, because that is what it is.

Failure mode:
- The model predicts private facts, sends cryptic messages, or behaves supernaturally.

## Gate 2 — LOSS MUST ARRIVE FROM LIFE, NOT FROM THE PREMISE

**Question:** Does mortality matter before Evan turns it into philosophy?

Pass condition:
- Ray's illness and death are specific, physical, undignified and loving.
- Evan tries technological preservation and discovers its emotional inadequacy.
- The audience understands that a perfect imitation would not answer the question he actually cares about.

Failure mode:
- Father's death exists only to motivate simulation obsession.

## Gate 3 — THE MARRIAGE MUST END WITHOUT A VILLAIN

**Question:** Can the central personal rupture be both avoidable and nobody's fault?

Pass condition:
- Claire articulates the real problem: Evan metabolizes uncertainty by leaving the room mentally.
- Evan sees that she is right and still cannot become someone else quickly enough.
- Their divorce contains affection.

Failure mode:
- “AI ruined the marriage.”

## Gate 4 — THE BRAIN PARALLEL MUST BECOME PHYSICAL

**Question:** Has Evan learned enough neuroscience to stop using “brain = computer” as a slogan?

Pass condition:
- He understands memory as distributed physical change, not stored files.
- He understands perception as active sampling and reconstruction.
- He understands there is no internal observer separate from the machinery.
- Dr. Shah corrects at least one of his sloppy analogies.

Failure mode:
- Mystical neuroscience or a lecture that validates him.

## Gate 5 — OPAQUE AI MUST RHYME WITH HUMAN THOUGHT

**Question:** Can the “neuralese” threshold feel uncanny without implying sentience has been scientifically solved?

Pass condition:
- Advanced models perform long reasoning in internal representations that cannot be faithfully serialized to language.
- Their verbal explanations are clearly summaries/translations.
- Evan notices his own experience has the same structure: thought precedes wording.

Failure mode:
- A machine announces “I am conscious.”

## Gate 6 — THE BRAIN–MACHINE BORDER MUST FAIL LOCALLY

**Question:** Can the movie make the biological/artificial boundary emotionally porous?

Pass condition:
- Julian receives a neural prosthesis after stroke.
- A machine-mediated function feels unquestionably like *Julian's* restored agency, not an external puppet.
- No one can identify the exact boundary where help becomes self.

Failure mode:
- Superhuman BCI spectacle.

## Gate 7 — “WHY NOW?” MUST EMERGE AS AN ANTHROPIC UNEASE, NOT PROOF

**Question:** Has Evan earned the simulation suspicion?

Pass condition:
- He frames the coincidence carefully: a person born near the transition from no synthetic minds/worlds to plausible synthetic minds/worlds may naturally overestimate the significance of their era.
- Julian or Mara states the obvious counterargument.
- Evan admits there is no proof.
- The question bothers him anyway.

Failure mode:
- Napkin math presented as decisive evidence.

## Gate 8 — EVAN MUST LET GO BEFORE HE DIES

**Question:** Can he finish his human arc without receiving an answer?

Pass condition:
- Mara tells him that metaphysics cannot substitute for presence.
- Evan stops trying to solve reality.
- He reconciles with Claire in a small, non-romantic way.
- He faces death wanting more life, not escape.

Failure mode:
- He dies happy because he “knows” there is an afterlife.

## Gate 9 — THE REVEAL MUST UNDERCUT ITS OWN GRANDEUR

**Question:** Can waking outside reality be funny, mundane and emotionally destabilizing?

Pass condition:
- Waiting room, ordinary chairs, tired people comparing lives.
- No exposition speech.
- Evan remembers by degrees.
- Previous lives are discussed like difficult trips, not prestige achievements.

Failure mode:
- Heaven, glowing beings, cosmic council, lore dump.

## Gate 10 — UTOPIA MUST EXPLAIN THE GAME WITHOUT JUSTIFYING PAIN

**Question:** Why would anyone choose mortality?

Pass condition:
- Outer life genuinely has no scarcity, involuntary death, class or coercion.
- It is not secretly evil.
- Its problem is reversible consequence: nothing has enough weight.
- Characters openly acknowledge that some inner lives contain horrors no one should romanticize.
- Choosing a bounded life remains optional.

Failure mode:
- “Suffering is good for you.”

## Gate 11 — THE REPLAY MUST COST HIM THE ANSWER

**Question:** Why end with “no AI”?

Pass condition:
- Evan recognizes that AI was one of the mechanisms by which his last life became self-aware of its frame.
- He still chooses to remove it.
- The audience understands he is choosing deeper uncertainty, not an easier world.
- He chooses forgetting.

Failure mode:
- The ending is a joke or arbitrary sequel hook.

## Final acceptance test

The screenplay passes only if these two interpretations can coexist until Evan wakes:

1. Evan is a mortal man using technological change to build a consoling metaphysics around grief.
2. Evan is a mortal man correctly noticing the conceptual shadow of a larger reality.

The film should not cheat in favor of interpretation 2 until after death.
